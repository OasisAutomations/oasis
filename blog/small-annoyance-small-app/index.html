Turning a daily annoyance into a small app

Most of what I build starts with something that bugs me every day. A car music player is a good example, and the same five steps apply to office work.

Most of what I build starts with a small irritation that happens every day. Not a big business problem, just a thing that's annoying enough that I keep noticing it.

Here's an example that has nothing to do with work: music in the car.

The annoyance

I like my own music files, played in order, with songs blending into each other the way a DJ mix does. The apps I tried either didn't crossfade, needed a subscription, had buttons too small to hit safely at a red light, or stopped playing the moment I switched over to maps. None of that is a disaster. It just bugged me on every drive.

The small app

So I built a simple player for myself, which I called Daniel Car. It does a short list of things:

  • Big controls you can hit without looking twice.
  • Crossfades between songs.
  • Keeps playing, and keeps crossfading, when the app is minimized and the phone is showing something else.
  • Works with the phone's normal media controls, so the lock screen and Bluetooth next/previous buttons work.

That third item took the most work. Phones are aggressive about pausing things running in the background, so the first version would stall between songs as soon as I switched apps. It took a few rounds of testing on real drives to get the handoff between tracks reliable. That's normal. The first version of anything I make is a rough draft.

The pattern I keep using

Whether it's a music player or a takeoff tool, the process is about the same:

  1. Write the annoyance down in one sentence. "Songs don't blend, and it stops when I open maps." If I can't say it in one sentence, I don't understand it yet.
  2. List the three to five things that would fix it. Not twenty. The short list keeps the app small enough to finish.
  3. Build the ugly version first. Get it working for me, on my own device, before thinking about how it looks.
  4. Use it for real. A week of actual use finds problems that no amount of clicking around at a desk will.
  5. Fix what bugs me, then stop. When it stops being annoying, it's done for now.

Why bother?

Because a tool that fits exactly how you work saves a little time and a little frustration every single day, and that adds up. The same approach works for office tasks: a checklist that fills itself in, a sheet that formats itself the way your boss wants, a reminder that shows up at the right moment. Most of those don't need a big software purchase. They need someone to take the annoyance seriously for an afternoon or two.

That's what Oasis Automations is about: small, practical tools for real daily friction. If there's one that bugs you every day, tell me about it.
